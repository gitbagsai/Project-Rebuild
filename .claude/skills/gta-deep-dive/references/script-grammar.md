# Script grammar

The narrator is a well-informed fan who has done the homework: precise, dry, a little funny, never hype. Sentences are short. Facts are sourced in the sentence. Jokes come *after* the fact, as a tag, never instead of it.

## 1. Find the spine before writing

From the idea, write one sentence of the form **"In [old thing], X. In [new thing], Y."** or **"[Game] was built around one question. [Other] asks a different one."** This is the video's thesis and its ending.

Examples:
- "GTA 5 is about what you can get away with. GTA 6 is about what you leave behind."
- "In GTA 4 the city was a backdrop. In GTA 5 it was a playground."
- "Every Rockstar police system tries to answer: how long should a crime follow you?"

Then list 5-9 **systems** (chapters) the idea touches. A system is something with rules the player feels: cops, money, cars, weapons, body, relationships, people, world, missions, online economy. Order them so each one causes the next (see Bridges).

## 2. Cold open (first 20-30 seconds)

- 4-7 paired contrasts, old then new, each under 12 words. Escalate from familiar to absurd.
- End the list on the strangest true item and immediately prove it ("That last one happened in Rockstar's own demo").
- One authority line from a real outlet or developer that states the thesis. Quote it exactly and attribute it.
- Method line: what you went through and that you counted/compared. ("So I went through every trailer and every preview, then counted...")
- Two open loops: one structural ("a lot of these connect"), one specific and withheld ("there's one thing GTA 5 lets you do that GTA 6 took away... we'll get there"). Pay both off.

No channel intro, no "welcome back", no subscribe ask in the opening.

## 3. Chapter = system

Open each chapter on its most noticeable item ("Let's start with the one everybody noticed"). Inside, run feature beats:

**Feature beat:** claim → old-game contrast → source/hedge → consequence or joke tag.

> Muscle fades if you stop training. *(claim)* GTA 5's fitness system was Michael doing yoga. *(contrast)* Previews say gains take three days in a row. *(source)* Rockstar invented skipping leg day. *(tag)*

Rules:
- One idea per sentence. Lists become one-word sentences ("Dump the gun. Dump the car.").
- Every number gets its comparison (600,000 animations vs about 55,000).
- Name the source in the sentence when the claim is surprising: "In Edge's demo...", "Rob Nelson told...", "According to Bloomberg's Jason Schreier...".
- Mark certainty honestly: confirmed by the developer / reported by previews / seen in footage / rumour. Never upgrade a rumour.
- Prefer a mini-story to a description when one exists (the spike strip, the dog). Stories need: who, what they were doing, the thing that went wrong, the punchline line.

## 4. Bridges

The last line of a chapter sets up the next system. Write the bridge first, then fill the chapter.

- Escape depends on looks → "Which is a problem. Because now, your body changes too."
- She's dressed as security → "What's she carrying?" → weapons.
- Your car now carries your guns → "So you'd better hope it has gas in it." → cars.
- Stealing cars is a job → "And you're going to need jobs." → money.

If two chapters can't be bridged, reorder them.

## 5. Callbacks

Plant 3-5 words or images early that will come back:
- A named mechanic ("Remember that word. It comes back.").
- The opening's strangest item (the stranger and the dog).
- An earlier chapter turning into a tool ("Remember chapter one? That's a disguise.").

Each callback is a reward. Space them through the video, densest in the ending.

## 6. Pace modules (use one or two, not all)

- **Fast round:** 10-15 items, each a noun phrase plus at most one qualifier ("Basketball, for the first time since San Andreas."). One shot and one label per item.
- **Rumour pile:** unconfirmed items, each with its exact status ("It showed up in 2022 test footage, but Rockstar hasn't confirmed it").
- **Flip side:** what was removed or got worse. Put the withheld open loop last.
- **Countdown:** "Five left." when the list is long, so the viewer knows the end is coming.

## 7. Ending

1. Return to the opening authority quote.
2. The thesis question pair.
3. One concrete callback per chapter in a rapid list ("The shirt a witness remembers. The weight from all those drive-throughs...").
4. Concede the risk honestly ("Is that worse? It could be.") with 2-3 specific "great until" lines.
5. Return to joy: the moments people remember are unplanned.
6. One comment question that asks the viewer to choose (not "let me know what you think").
7. Final line: a date or a next step. No long outro.

## 8. Write for the picture

Next to every sentence, the script draft keeps a **visual note**: the exact moment the viewer should see. If a sentence cannot be shown, decide on purpose: card, recreated UI, stamp, or rewrite the sentence around a moment you *can* show. See `sourcing.md` → "Write and source in one loop".

## 9. Length and density

- Long form: 15-30 minutes, 180-210 spoken words per minute.
- Mid form: 8-12 minutes, 3-5 systems.
- Every 60-90 seconds something must change: a chapter, a story, a pace module, a callback, or a reveal.

## 10. Tone checklist

- Dry jokes from the facts, never random. ("It's the only anti-theft system in history that punishes you for obeying the speed limit.")
- First-person sparingly and only for judgement or effort ("I've thought about this more than is healthy").
- Speak to the player ("you") for mechanics.
- No hype words (insane, mind-blowing, crazy) in narration; the facts do that.
- No filler transitions ("Moving on", "Next up"): use bridges.
