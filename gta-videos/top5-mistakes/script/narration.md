# Top 5 Mistakes to Avoid in GTA 6 (narration only, draft v1)

Read at a natural pace; pauses are breaths. Record to `audio/vo.wav` (WAV, 48 kHz if possible). Small wording changes while recording are fine; the edit follows the recording.


## Cold open

In GTA 5, you die, you wake up outside a hospital, and you carry on.
In GTA 6, the cash in your pocket is just… gone.
In GTA 5, you lose the cops and they forget you exist.
In GTA 6, they remember your shirt.
In GTA 5, you can replay any mission from the pause menu.
In GTA 6, you can't. Not even after the credits.
And there's one mission in Chapter 1 that takes your car, your house, and your wallet, and you can start it by accident.
Rockstar North's Rob Nelson told Famitsu that Jason and Lucia keep bank accounts alongside the cash they carry, and that getting wasted or busted puts that cash at risk.
So I went through the guides written since launch, and the money, police and save systems behind them, and boiled it down to five.
These five feed into each other. Make one, and you're halfway to the next.
And number one is that mission. We'll get there.

## Number 5: Walking around with your cash

Number five. Walking around with your cash.
GTA 6 splits your money in two. There's what's in the bank, and there's what's in your pocket.
Rob a store, and the money goes in your pocket.
And pocket money is the money you lose.
Get wasted or busted, and according to every guide I've read, that cash is gone. Not dropped. Not recoverable. Gone.
Picture it. You rob a liquor store. Then a gas station. Then a second liquor store, because the first one went so well.
You're carrying the best afternoon of your life. Then a guy in a pickup clips you at an intersection, you come off the bike, and the cops arrive before you stand up.
Every dollar of that afternoon: gone.
The bank is safe. Death doesn't touch it. Arrest doesn't touch it.
GTA 5 never really had this problem. Your money just lived in a bank account. Three of them, actually.
So the habit you bring from GTA 5 is to stop thinking about money. That's the habit that costs you.
The fix is boring: after a robbery, a job or a big sale, bank it before you do anything else.
Because the fastest way to lose that cash… is getting caught.

## Number 4: Thinking a new hat fools the cops

Number four. Thinking a new hat fools the cops.
GTA 6's police don't just count stars. Previews call it Heat: icons under your wanted level that show what the police actually know.
Your outfit. Your face. Your car.
And it needs a trigger. A witness calling it in, an alarm, a camera.
When the stars turn red, the cops can't see you, but they're looking for anyone who matches the description.
That's a real change from GTA 5, where a cop seeing you was the whole investigation. Here, the information has to reach them first, and it can be wrong, or incomplete, or about your car instead of you.
Which is where the mistake comes in.
In GTA 5, the move was to drive out of the circle and wait.
In GTA 6, people grab a hat, put on sunglasses and walk past the cop like nothing happened.
According to preview coverage, that doesn't work. A hat and shades don't change the description. A full outfit change does.
Changing your hair reportedly clears your face, but not your clothes.
So keep a spare outfit in the trunk. Previews say that's now an actual feature. And if there's a camera, wear a mask before the crime, not after.
The cops can forget your shirt.
Your reputation doesn't forget anything.

## Number 3: Living in the Psycho tier

Number three. Living in the Psycho tier.
GTA 6 tracks what kind of criminal you are. Guides list five Criminal Profile levels, from Professional at one end to Irredeemable at the other.
Four of those levels move. Do some clean jobs, calm down for a while, and your profile drifts back.
That's the system working as intended. Have a bad day, have a good week, and the game lets you be a different person again. Mostly.
Irredeemable doesn't. Guides agree it's permanent, for that character, for that playthrough.
The good news: one bad afternoon won't get you there. Polygon's description is that one rampage isn't enough. You have to keep it up for hours.
The bad news is that GTA 5 trained us to do exactly that for hours.
According to guides, once you're Irredeemable, police shoot on sight, surrendering stops being an option, and you lose a passive way of building Focus.
How it changes the story, nobody's fully mapped yet. I'm not going to guess.
If you want to go full Psycho, and honestly, fair, make a manual save first.
Because Irredeemable is permanent… unless you have a save from before it.
Do you?

## Number 2: Trusting the autosave

Number two. Trusting the autosave.
GTA 6 gives you fifteen manual save slots. And one autosave.
Remember that number. Fifteen. It comes back.
Here's the part that catches people. There's no mission replay.
GTA 5 let you replay any story mission from the pause menu.
GTA 6, according to every save guide out there, does not, not even after the credits. If you want a mission back, you need a manual save from before it.
And you can't manual save in the middle of a mission.
One more trap: you only get one autosave slot. Guides warn that loading an older save can overwrite it. So before you load anything, save where you are now, in a spare slot.
Here's the routine I'd use. One slot per chapter, saved at the start, and never overwritten. One rolling slot you save to every session. And one spare, for right before anything that looks important.
Fifteen slots sounds like a lot. Across a whole story, with no replays, it isn't.
The habit to build: before every big mission, new slot.
And there's one mission you really want a save in front of.

## Fast round: four quick ones

Before number one, four quick ones.
Banks and loan stores, early. Guides agree the cash has dye packs that go off if you get too far from the building, and the money's ruined.
You need the Dye Pack Jammer, and that comes from the Chapter 3 mission Credit Check. Until then, stick to stores.
Stolen cars. Early on, they aren't yours to keep. From Chapter 2, guides say you can register one, for a fee.
Cameras. A mask before the crime means CCTV has nothing useful to send.
Settings. Before your first big chase, spend two minutes on camera speed, aim sensitivity and subtitles. Every guide says it. Almost nobody does it.
And buying a new personal car late in Chapter 1.
That last one's a hint.

## Number 1: Starting Tourist Trap unprepared

Number one. Starting Tourist Trap unprepared.
Tourist Trap is the point of no return in Chapter 1. Start it, and guides say you're locked into a three-mission run straight into Chapter 2.
When it's over, you lose Jason's personal vehicles. Every upgrade on them. Even the Ultimate Edition cars.
You lose the house. The cash in your wallet. Your checking account balance.
Remember mistake five? The pocket cash? This is where it hurts most.
What you keep, according to guides: a capped amount of family savings, your relationship with Lucia, and your unique weapons.
Several guides also list Chapter 1 side activities you can miss for good once Tourist Trap starts. Check a missables list before you go, because the game doesn't hand you one.
So that car you bought late in Chapter 1? That was the hint.
Before you start Tourist Trap: finish the Chapter 1 side activities you care about, spend the money you'd lose on things that last, and make a manual save.
Remember fifteen? This is what one of those slots is for.

## Ending

Rob Nelson told Famitsu that getting wasted or busted puts your cash at risk. That's the whole game in one sentence.
GTA 5 asked how fast you could get back into the action. GTA 6 asks what you're still carrying when you do.
The cash in your pocket. The shirt a witness remembers. The hat that fooled nobody. The profile you can't take back. The save you didn't make. The car you bought too late.
See? They connect.
Is that more punishing than GTA 5? Yes. It's great until you lose twenty minutes of loot to a fender bender. It's great until the cops recognise your favourite jacket.
And if you play the way GTA 5 taught you, the first ten hours of GTA 6 will feel like the game is against you.
It isn't. It just keeps receipts.
But nobody ever told a good GTA story about a mistake they avoided. It's always the one they made.
So which one got you: the pocket cash, or Tourist Trap?
Bank your money. See you in Leonida.
